## 1. Problem Context & Motivation

GlassFit has established a functional 3D visualization foundation:
- Parametric extrusion models generate realistic multi-panel windows with structural profiles, mullions, jambs, and sills.
- MS-02 and fix-MS-02 implemented 4-point perspective plane fitting using CSS `matrix3d()` homography, edge-to-edge 3D perspective camera projection with diverging rays, and full sidebar slider configurability (3D Yaw, 3D Pitch, Width, Height, Panel Count, Sill Toggle).
- MS-03 implemented point-based manual occlusion masking for architectural protrusions (columns, piers, and beams).

However, when reviewing real-world client photographs (such as rough-construction openings with unfinished concrete walls and variable lighting), the 3D model frequently **looks out of place**:

1. **Lighting Dissonance (Blown-Out White Highlights vs. Dim Under-Exposure):**
   - In `ProductModelRenderer`, light intensities are statically high (`ambient: 0.45`, `main: 2.0`, `bevel: 1.4`). Summing over 4.0 in directional irradiance with ACESFilmic tone mapping washes out powder-coated white aluminum frames (`0xf4f1ea`), blowing out highlights into chalky `#ffffff` and flattening subtle bevels and chamfers.
   - In low-light or unevenly lit rooms, the window frame remains unnaturally bright, appearing like an illuminated sticker pasted over a dark photograph.

2. **White Balance & Color Temperature Disconnect:**
   - Real-world indoor photos exhibit distinct ambient color casts (e.g., warm beige plaster, incandescent tungsten at 2700K, or cool overcast daylight at 6500K).
   - The Three.js lighting setup currently lerps ambient color 50% toward pure white (`0xffffff`) and uses static material albedos, ignoring diffuse environmental bounce from surrounding walls and floors.

3. **Image Quality & Sensor Grain Discrepancy (CGI "Uncanny Valley"):**
   - Smartphone and compressed user photographs contain natural ISO sensor noise, optical softness, and compression artifacts.
   - The WebGL renderer outputs mathematically pristine, noiseless vector pixels. This high-frequency sharpness contrast instantly betrays the model as an artificial overlay on lower-quality or high-noise photos.
   - Conversely, when applying uniform CSS blur filters, high-resolution DSLR photos suffer unneeded loss of detail.

4. **Milky Opaque Glass Occluding the Aperture:**
   - Clear glass is currently defined with `opacity: 0.50` and `transmission: 0.55` with a pastel cyan tint (`0xd8edf5`).
   - When fitted into a window opening, this semi-opaque veil obscures the outdoor sky, trees, or construction scene behind the aperture, replacing reality with cloudy plastic.

5. **Lack of Contact Occlusion and Aperture Reveal Shadows:**
   - Real window frames installed into rough openings exhibit contact ambient occlusion (a subtle 1-2px contact darkening along the frame boundary) and aperture reveal shading (shadows cast by the top wall header or side jamb onto the frame).
   - Current drop-shadow filters apply a diffuse card shadow outside the bounding box, rather than realistic contact and cavity ambient occlusion.

### Preservation Directive: DO NOT DOWNGRADE

The current implementation serves as an approved baseline. MS-04 must strictly preserve:
- Exact 4-point perspective plane fitting boundary alignment without detached canvas margins (from MS-02 / fix-MS-02).
- Edge-to-edge 3D perspective camera projection with diverging rays that render jamb reveals, bevels, and sill depth.
- Full real-time sidebar configurability (Yaw, Pitch, Dimensions, Finishes, Sills, Panels) without locked state.
- Seamless compatibility with MS-03 point-based manual occlusion masks and YOLOv8s-seg auto-detected foreground objects.
- 100% snapshot export parity between live DOM viewports and high-resolution exported snapshots.
- Graceful degradation: if image analysis is unavailable or turned off, the renderer defaults cleanly to the existing verified baseline.

## 4. Technical Specification

### 4.1 FastAPI Computer Vision Microservice Refinements

**File:** `fastapi-service/brightness.py`

Refine the image analysis pipeline to extract high-fidelity environmental characteristics without increasing inference latency:

1. **Dynamic Range & Exposure Profiling:**
   - Compute luminance histogram percentiles (`p5`, `p50`, `p95`) in CIELAB lightness ($L^*$) to detect high-contrast backlighting, deep indoor shadows, or washed-out flash photography.
   - Formulate normalized scene exposure $E_{scene} \in [0.65, 1.35]$:
     $$E_{scene} = \text{clamp}\left(\frac{\text{median}(L^*)}{50.0} \times 0.90 + \frac{\text{mean}(L^*)}{50.0} \times 0.10, 0.65, 1.35\right)$$

2. **Ambient Environmental Irradiance (Bounce Tint):**
   - Segment mid-tone room surfaces (excluding light sources and deep specular reflections) between the 20th and 80th lightness percentiles.
   - Extract the normalized ambient color vector $\mathbf{C}_{ambient} = [R, G, B]_{norm}$ and compute its Kelvin-correlated color temperature shift (warmth factor $W \in [-1.0, 1.0]$ and green-magenta tint $T \in [-1.0, 1.0]$).

3. **Sensor Noise & Grain Estimation:**
   - Apply a $3 \times 3$ Laplacian high-pass filter on the luminance channel.
   - Compute the standard deviation of high-frequency residuals in flat, non-edge regions (using a Sobel edge mask to ignore real architectural contours).
   - Output normalized grain intensity $G_{scene} \in [0.0, 0.20]$ and sharpness factor $S_{scene} \in [0.0, 1.50]$.

4. **Directional Light Estimation & Dominance:**
   - Compute spatial quadrant luminance gradients (top-bottom, left-right).
   - Evaluate directional confidence $\kappa \in [0.0, 1.0]$ based on shadow contrast. When confidence is low (diffuse overcast lighting), directional light intensity is attenuated in favor of ambient irradiance.

```python
class SuggestedModelAdjustments(TypedDict):

    contrast: float            # [0.80, 1.20]
```

### 4.2 Three.js Dynamic PBR Scene Lighting & Tone Mapping

**File:** `src/lib/visualization/modelRenderer.ts`

The current hardcoded light setup washes out white materials and ignores ambient bounces. The updated `applyLighting` pipeline modulates lights dynamically:

```typescript
export interface LightingAnalysisPayload {
  mean_rgb: [number, number, number];
  ambient_rgb: [number, number, number];
  ambient_hex: string;
  contrast: number;
  saturation: number;
  warmth: number;
  tint: number;
  temperature: "cool" | "neutral" | "warm";
  sharpness: number;
  noise: number;
  light_direction: { x: number; y: number };
  suggested: {
    brightness: number;
    contrast: number;
    saturation: number;
    color_mix: number;
    blur_px: number;
    grain: number;
    shadow_opacity: number;
    exposure_bias?: number;
    directional_intensity?: number;
    ambient_intensity?: number;
  };
}
```

#### Lighting Calibration Rules:

1. **Prevent White Powder-Coat Washout:**
   - Limit `mainLight.intensity` to a maximum of $1.55$ (reduced from $2.00$).
   - Limit `bevelLight.intensity` to $0.85$ (reduced from $1.40$).
   - Set `ambientLight.intensity` within the range $[0.28, 0.42]$.
   - Total scene illuminance is capped so that a pure white diffuser with albedo $0.92$ does not saturate the ACESFilmic tone mapper beyond the knee threshold.

2. **Environmental Bounce Irradiance:**
   - Set `ambientLight.color` to match $\mathbf{C}_{ambient}$ with a $30\%$ neutral white floor:
     $$\mathbf{C}_{final\_ambient} = \mathbf{C}_{ambient} \times 0.70 + [1.0, 1.0, 1.0] \times 0.30$$
   - Key light color picks up subtle scene warmth:
     $$\mathbf{C}_{key} = [1.0, 1.0, 1.0] \times 0.75 + \mathbf{C}_{ambient} \times 0.25$$
   - Fill light receives complementary bounce tint to create natural architectural lighting depth.

3. **Tone Mapping & Exposure Calibration:**
   - Configure WebGL tone mapping:
     ```typescript
     this.renderer.toneMapping = THREE.ACESFilmicToneMapping;
     const baseExposure = THREE.MathUtils.clamp(
       (meanLuminance / 128.0) * 0.95 + 0.10,
       0.75,
       1.20
     );
     this.renderer.toneMappingExposure = baseExposure;
     ```

### 4.4 Sensor Grain & High/Low Quality Noise Synthesis

**File:** `src/lib/visualization/noiseGenerator.ts` (NEW)

To eliminate the visual disconnect between noiseless WebGL pixels and grainy mobile photographs, MS-04 introduces calibrated grain injection:

1. **Live DOM Rendering:**
   - Inject an inline SVG filter definition into the workspace container with an `id="glassfit-grain-filter"`.
   - The SVG filter utilizes `<feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.75" numOctaves="3" />` combined with `<feColorMatrix type="matrix" ... />` to generate high-frequency monochromatic luminance grain.
   - The grain opacity is dynamically controlled via CSS `mix-blend-mode: overlay` at an intensity proportional to `lighting.suggested.grain` ($1.5\% \text{ to } 18.0\%$).

2. **Snapshot Export Rendering (Canvas 2D Parity):**
   - In `src/lib/visualization/noiseGenerator.ts`, provide `applyNoiseToCanvas(targetCtx, width, height, grainIntensity)`:
     ```typescript
     export function applyNoiseToCanvas(
       ctx: CanvasRenderingContext2D,
       width: number,
       height: number,
       grainIntensity: number
     ): void {
       if (grainIntensity <= 0.01) return;

       const noiseCanvas = document.createElement("canvas");
       noiseCanvas.width = width;
       noiseCanvas.height = height;
       const noiseCtx = noiseCanvas.getContext("2d");
       if (!noiseCtx) return;

       const imgData = noiseCtx.createImageData(width, height);
       const data = imgData.data;
       const factor = grainIntensity * 255;

       for (let i = 0; i < data.length; i += 4) {
         const noise = (Math.random() - 0.5) * factor;
         data[i] = 128 + noise;     // Red
         data[i + 1] = 128 + noise; // Green
         data[i + 2] = 128 + noise; // Blue
         data[i + 3] = Math.round(grainIntensity * 160); // Alpha
       }

       noiseCtx.putImageData(imgData, 0, 0);

       ctx.save();
       ctx.globalCompositeOperation = "overlay";
       ctx.drawImage(noiseCanvas, 0, 0);
       ctx.restore();
     }
     ```

3. **Adaptive Sharpness & Blur Falloff:**
   - If the room photograph has low optical sharpness ($S_{scene} < 0.40$), apply a subtle Gaussian roll-off ($0.15\text{px to } 0.35\text{px}$) to the rendered model layer.
   - If the room photograph is crisp ($S_{scene} \ge 0.70$), zero blur is applied, preserving sharp extrusion chamfers.

### 4.5 Perimeter Ambient Occlusion & Aperture Reveal Shading

**File:** `src/lib/visualization/contactShadow.ts` (NEW)

To seat the product naturally inside a wall opening rather than floating above it:

1. **Perimeter Ambient Occlusion (AO) Crevice:**
   - Render a subtle inset/outset gradient along the outer perimeter quadrilateral of the window frame.
   - The crevice spans $2\text{px to } 4\text{px}$ with a soft quadratic falloff ($rgba(15, 20, 30, \alpha)$ where $\alpha \approx 0.35$).
   - This produces the micro-shadow that naturally occurs where aluminum profiles press against rough concrete or plastered drywall.

2. **Aperture Reveal Cast Shadow:**
   - Real wall openings possess physical depth ($100\text{mm to } 200\text{mm}$).
   - Based on `light_direction` from OpenCV analysis:
     - If light originates from the top ($\text{direction.y} > 0$), the upper wall header casts an inner shadow down onto the top frame rail and upper glass pane ($4\text{px to } 12\text{px}$ gradient).
     - If light originates from the left ($\text{direction.x} > 0$), the left wall jamb casts an inner shadow across the left frame stile.
   - This inner reveal gradient is rendered directly on top of the frame/glass layer before occlusion compositing, rooting the window deeply inside the wall cavity.

### 4.6 Edge Integration & Sub-Pixel Anti-Aliasing

**File:** `src/lib/visualization/perspectiveTransform.ts`

When warping the 3D model through CSS `matrix3d()` or the snapshot affine triangle mesh:
- Apply a $0.5\text{px}$ soft alpha falloff along the outermost boundary vertices.
- This prevents hard stair-stepped aliasing artifacts and blends the outer frame boundary organically into the textured wall pixels behind it.

## 7. Risk Assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme overexposure or darkness in user photos skews calculations | Medium | Medium | Implement strict clamping boundaries ($E_{scene} \in [0.65, 1.35]$, grain $\le 0.18$) so lighting never degenerates to pure black or pure white |
